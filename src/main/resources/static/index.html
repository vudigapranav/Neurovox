How to use Neurovox

Eight steps,
start to finish.

The recorder walks you through each step on screen, with a progress bar at the top and a Back button on every step. This is the whole journey at a glance.

  1. Know what it measures

    Pitch, cycle-to-cycle timing (jitter), cycle loudness (shimmer) and voice-to-noise balance (HNR), computed in Java on this computer. It is a measurement tool, not a diagnosis, stage estimate or treatment advice.

  2. Prepare a quiet room

    Close windows, switch off fans and music, sit upright and keep the microphone 20–30 cm from your mouth. Use the same microphone every time so sessions can be compared.

  3. Allow the microphone

    Press “Allow microphone” and accept the browser’s prompt. Say “aah”: the level bar should reach the green zone and the orb should move. If access is blocked, the page shows how to unblock it in your browser.

  4. Record the four tasks

    A steady “aah” (5 s), “pa-ta-ka” as fast as you can (6 s), a short passage read aloud (20 s), and free speech (30 s). Instructions and the text to read stay on screen while you record.

  5. Start, stop, replay, retry, save

    Press Start: a 3-second countdown, then recording. Stop early once the minimum time has passed, or let the timer finish. “Review this recording” lets you play it back, record again, or save it. Java checks each saved take and tells you how to fix a take that is too quiet, clipped or not voiced.

  6. Let Java process the recordings

    After the fourth take, the Java server resamples each recording to 16 kHz, tracks pitch, finds every vocal cycle in the steady vowel and computes the measurements. It takes a few seconds; nothing leaves this computer.

  7. Read the measurements

    The report shows each number with a plain name, its technical name and what it means — without invented “normal ranges”. Compare against your own earlier sessions. The experimental research indicator is shown only if validation supports it; currently it does not, and the report explains why.

  8. Review history, delete data

    History lists every session with a trend chart. Unfinished sessions can be continued. You can delete one session, or all local data, at any time.

The session

Four tasks.
One sitting.

Each task asks the voice to do something different. Together they take about a minute of speaking, grouped into one session you can review later.

5 s 6 s 20 s 30 s
  1. 01

    Sustained vowel

    Hold a steady “aah” at a comfortable pitch and loudness.

    Pitch, cycle-to-cycle timing and amplitude, voice-to-noise balance. The only task used by the research indicator.

    ≈ 5 s
  2. 02

    Syllable repetition

    Repeat “pa-ta-ka” as quickly and clearly as you can.

    Descriptive measurements of rhythm and articulation.

    ≈ 6 s
  3. 03

    Reading passage

    Read a short fixed passage aloud at your usual pace.

    Descriptive measurements of connected speech.

    ≈ 20 s
  4. 04

    Free speech

    Talk about anything — your day, a place, a recipe.

    Descriptive measurements of spontaneous speech.

    ≈ 30 s

Method

From sound
to signal.

Every measurement is computed by code the team wrote and can explain, then checked against reference measurements before it is trusted.

  1. 01 · Capture

    Raw voice, as it is

    The browser captures mono audio at its real sample rate and hands it to the local Java server.

  2. 02 · Condition

    Clean and check

    Java removes DC offset, resamples to 16 kHz and rejects silence, clipping or bad durations.

  3. 03 · Measure

    Find each cycle

    Pitch is tracked with autocorrelation and YIN, then individual vocal pulses are located for jitter, shimmer and HNR.

  4. 04 · Report

    Plain language

    Results are explained simply, with the technical names kept alongside for review.

Boundaries

What it is,
and what it isn’t.

Neurovox is

  • A Java Programming course end project
  • An acoustic measurement tool with explainable formulas
  • Local: recordings and results stay on this computer

Neurovox is not

  • A diagnosis of Parkinson’s disease or any condition
  • An estimate of disease stage or severity
  • Medical or treatment advice

Any classifier output is an experimental research indicator from the sustained vowel only, and it is shown only if validation supports it. At present the validation does not support it, so it is not shown. If you have concerns about your health, speak with a clinician.
